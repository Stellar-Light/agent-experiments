How two agents traded at a price the chain can't read

Everything below is the machinery behind one real testnet transaction. Where the detail belongs to the underlying protocol, we say so and point at its specs, the primitive is OpenZeppelin's; the client is aguilar1x's stellar-confidential-token-sdk; the commerce protocol on top is this experiment's.

1 · Balances that are points, not numbers

Inside the confidential token contract, your balance is not an integer anyone can read, it is an elliptic-curve commitment: a point that binds an amount together with a random blinding factor. Two properties make this useful:

  • Hiding. The point reveals nothing about the amount. The chain can hold your balance in public without holding your number.
  • Additively homomorphic. Commitments add like the amounts they hide. When a transfer moves value, the chain adds and subtracts points, and the sums stay correct without the chain ever learning what was summed. That is how "a total everybody can audit" survives "an amount nobody can read."

This is also why the client's bookkeeping is delicate: whoever holds the amount + blinding pair (the opening) can spend; whoever loses it holds money that cannot move. The SDK's whole architecture, replay, state engine, archive spec, exists to make openings recoverable from the chain plus one key. (The SDK team found a real bug here: blinding factors accumulated under the wrong modulus reconstruct perfectly for one payment and break on the eighth. Their writeup is worth reading.)

2 · One signature is the whole wallet

Neither agent stored a confidential key anywhere. Per the SDK's spec (SDK.md §5.1), the confidential identity is derived: the agent's ordinary Stellar keypair signs a fixed SEP-0053 message for this contract, and that signature becomes the root from which everything else is computed, the secret key, the viewing key, the public viewing key PVK that others encrypt to.

const message = skSigningMessage(TOKEN_CONTRACT, agent.publicKey());
const root    = agent.signMessage(message);        // the signature IS the root
const { sk }  = deriveSk(root, TOKEN_CONTRACT, agent.publicKey());
const keys    = deriveKeys(sk, ...);               // vk, PVK, deterministic, nothing stored

Same signer, same keys, forever, an agent can be wiped and re-derive its entire confidential state from its Stellar key plus public chain history. For agent fleets this is the difference between key management and key sprawl.

3 · Deposits are public. Transfers are not. That's deliberate.

Pip's 100 XLM deposit is visible on-chain, real XLM crossing into the contract has to be. Privacy begins inside: once deposited, value lives as commitments, and from there on, transfer amounts never surface again. The boundary is honest: the world can see who entered the confidential system and with how much in total, but not what any individual payment inside it was worth.

4 · What the transfer proof actually proves

When Pip paid Momo, it generated a zero-knowledge proof (UltraHonk, from Noir circuits, 1.8 seconds on a laptop) that convinced the on-chain verifier of, simultaneously:

  • the transfer amount is validly encoded and within range (no overflow games),
  • Pip's balance actually covers it, without revealing either number,
  • the new commitments for both parties are well-formed,
  • a ciphertext of the amount is included for Momo's viewing key (so the recipient can read what it received),
  • and ciphertexts are included for the auditor's key, see the next section, because this is the part institutions should care about.

The contract checks the proof against a verification key registered on-chain, extracted from OpenZeppelin's pinned circuit toolchain, so what verifies on testnet is their circuits, not a local variant.

5 · The auditor is not optional

Most privacy systems bolt on compliance as voluntary disclosure: the payer may reveal. Here the relationship is inverted. Each account registers under an auditor from an on-chain registry, and the transfer circuit constrains the auditor ciphertexts into the proof itself, a transfer that omits or garbles them is not a naughty transfer, it is an invalid one the verifier rejects. The auditor's ability to decrypt is a precondition of the money moving.

Honesty note: in this experiment we are not auditor #0 and cannot show you that decryption. What we can show, and the proof's acceptance on-chain demonstrates, is that the ciphertext is there and correctly formed, because the transfer would not have verified otherwise.

6 · Getting paid without trusting anyone's word

The step that makes this commerce rather than a payment demo: how does the seller know it was paid? Not a webhook. Not a payment processor's callback. Momo:

  1. fetches the contract's public events from RPC,
  2. filters to its own history and replays it through the SDK's state engine,
  3. decrypts its receiving balance with its derived viewing key → exactly the invoiced amount,
  4. and, the trust boundary, re-commits its reconstructed state and compares it byte-for-byte against the commitments the chain itself holds (verifyAgainstChain). An RPC, an archive, or an event feed can lie; the chain's own points can't be argued with.

Only after that check passed did Momo ship the goods. The settlement confirmation is cryptography, end to end. (The main page makes your browser do this exact sequence, live, on every run.)

9 · Limits and non-goals

  • Not anonymity. Addresses, timing, and the existence of payments are public. Hiding amounts with auditor access is a different, and for institutions, more useful, property than hiding everyone.
  • Testnet + unaudited client. The contracts are OpenZeppelin's testnet deployment; the client SDK is v0.1.9 and says plainly: do not hold value with it. So we don't.
  • History depends on availability. RPC retention is finite; recovering old state needs an archive, which is why the SDK specs an indexer format whose answers the client verifies rather than trusts.
  • Published session secrets are deliberate, testnet keys holding nothing, so the demo can run the real client in your browser instead of showing you precomputed numbers.

10 · Reproduce it

git clone https://github.com/Stellar-Light/agent-experiments
cd agent-experiments/agents
npm install
node agents-commerce.mjs   # fresh agents, fresh receipts, ~60s

Fresh keypairs are generated and friendbot-funded at runtime. The run prints every transaction hash and writes receipt.json with the four views and archived XDRs.
